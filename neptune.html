A world of its own

Meet Neptune

Neptune is the most distant recognized major planet from the Sun. Its deep blue appearance comes from atmospheric gases and haze, while sunlight is nearly a thousand times fainter there than at Earth.

Neptune remains dynamic despite the weak sunlight. Winds can exceed 2,000 km/h, and dark storms appear and disappear. Voyager 2 observed the Great Dark Spot in 1989; it later vanished.

Neptune takes almost 165 Earth years to orbit the Sun. Its large moon Triton travels backward and may have been captured from the Kuiper Belt. Internal heat helps power Neptune’s active atmosphere.

Exploring Neptune.

MISSION HISTORY

Voyager 2

Voyager 2 made the only close flyby in 1989, observing its storms, rings, and moons. Hubble tracks changing weather, while JWST uses infrared light to examine clouds and rings.

Moons and companions

Neptune has 16 known moons. Triton is the largest and orbits backward, a clue that Neptune captured it rather than forming alongside the planet.
