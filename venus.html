A world of its own

Meet Venus

Venus is close to Earth in size, but its carbon-dioxide-rich atmosphere traps heat in a runaway greenhouse effect. Surface temperatures reach about 465°C beneath crushing pressure.

Sulfuric-acid clouds hide the surface. Radar has revealed volcanic plains, high plateaus, and thousands of volcanic features. Scientists continue to investigate whether Venus is volcanically active today.

Venus rotates slowly in the opposite direction from most planets, so its day is longer than its 225-day year. Fast upper-atmosphere winds circle the planet; the dense air keeps temperatures extreme across the surface.

MISSION HISTORY

Venera

Soviet Venera probes made the first landings on another planet. NASA’s Magellan mapped Venus with radar from orbit. JAXA’s Akatsuki studies cloud layers and weather.

Moons of Venus.

Moons and companions

Venus has no natural moons. Its slow backward rotation is one of the Solar System’s most unusual planetary motions.
