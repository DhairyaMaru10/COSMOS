A world of its own

Meet Uranus

Methane absorbs red light in Uranus’ atmosphere, giving the planet a blue-green appearance. Beneath its clouds lies a hot dense interior rich in water, ammonia, and methane around a rocky core.

Uranus’ axial tilt is about 98 degrees, so it rolls around the Sun. Each pole experiences decades of daylight followed by decades of darkness, producing unusual seasons. It also has faint rings and an offset magnetic field.

Voyager 2 remains the only spacecraft to visit Uranus, flying past in 1986. Modern telescopes track its changing clouds and seasons during its 84-year orbit.

Moons of Uranus.

Moons and companions

Uranus has 29 known moons. The five largest are Miranda, Ariel, Umbriel, Titania, and Oberon; many moons take names from literature.
