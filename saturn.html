A world of its own

Meet Saturn

Saturn’s rings consist mostly of water-ice particles, from fine grains to house-sized chunks. They span a vast distance yet are remarkably thin, with gaps and waves shaped by gravity and moons.

Saturn has no solid surface; hydrogen and helium transition into deep fluid layers. Fast winds circle the planet, and a persistent six-sided jet stream sits at its north pole. Its average density is lower than water’s.

Titan has a thick nitrogen atmosphere and lakes of methane. Enceladus ejects water-rich plumes from a subsurface ocean. Cassini-Huygens transformed our knowledge of these moons and Saturn’s rings.

Pioneer 11

Pioneer 11 and Voyager made early close encounters. Cassini orbited from 2004 to 2017; its Huygens probe landed on Titan in 2005 and returned surface data.

Moons and companions

Saturn’s 274 confirmed moons include Titan and Enceladus. Its rings appear to vanish from Earth about every 15 years when viewed edge-on.
