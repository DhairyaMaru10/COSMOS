A world of its own

Meet Jupiter

Jupiter is the largest planet, more massive than all the other planets combined. Made mainly of hydrogen and helium, it has no solid surface; pressure deep inside compresses the gas into fluid layers.

Jet streams shape cloud belts and zones, while the Great Red Spot is a long-lived storm. Jupiter’s powerful magnetic field creates intense radiation and auroras and interacts with volcanic Io.

The Galilean moons are worlds of their own: volcanic Io, icy Europa with a likely subsurface ocean, Ganymede—the largest moon—and ancient, cratered Callisto.

People, probes, and discoveries

Exploring Jupiter.

MISSION HISTORY

Voyager

Voyager revealed active volcanism and faint rings. Galileo orbited Jupiter from 1995 to 2003. Juno has studied the planet’s gravity, magnetic field, deep atmosphere, and auroras since 2016.

Moons and companions

Jupiter has 115 officially recognized moons. Ganymede is the only moon known to generate its own magnetic field.
