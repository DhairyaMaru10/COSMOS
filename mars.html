A world of its own

Meet Mars

Iron minerals in Mars’ dust have oxidized, giving the planet its red colour. Dry river channels, ancient lake deposits, and water-altered minerals show that liquid water once shaped the surface.

Olympus Mons rises about 22 km above its surroundings, and Valles Marineris stretches thousands of kilometres. Water ice remains in polar caps and underground, while thin air makes surface liquid water unstable today.

Rovers look for evidence of past habitable environments and possible ancient microbial life. Viking began surface exploration; Curiosity studies Gale Crater, and Perseverance explores Jezero Crater while caching samples.

A family of worlds

Moons of Mars.

Moons and companions

Phobos and Deimos are small, irregular moons. Phobos orbits in about seven hours and is slowly moving inward. A Martian sunset can look blue near the Sun.
