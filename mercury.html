A world of its own

Meet Mercury

Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun. Its year lasts just 88 Earth days, but a sunrise-to-sunrise solar day lasts 176 Earth days because it rotates slowly.

Mercury’s heavily cratered surface includes the vast Caloris basin and long cliffs formed as the interior cooled. Its large iron-rich core makes it unusually dense for its size.

A nearly absent atmosphere—an exosphere of oxygen, sodium, hydrogen, helium, and potassium—cannot hold heat. Daytime can reach 430°C, while nights fall near −180°C. Polar shadows may shelter water ice.

People, probes, and discoveries

Exploring Mercury.

MISSION HISTORY

Mariner 10

Mariner 10 returned the first close views. MESSENGER orbited from 2011–2015 and mapped the surface and polar ice. ESA-JAXA’s BepiColombo is on its way to study Mercury with two orbiters.

Moons of Mercury.

Moons and companions

Mercury has no moons. Its proximity to the Sun makes stable satellite orbits difficult. At some locations, the Sun appears to pause and briefly reverse across the sky.

Worth remembering

Quick wonders.

  • Three rotations for every two orbits
  • Caloris is about 1,550 km wide
  • second densest planet
  • exosphere includes sodium
  • permanently shadowed craters can preserve ice.
